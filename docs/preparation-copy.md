## Keeping shrimp cold

Bring an insulated cooler with ice or cold packs for pickup. Refrigerate promptly at 40°F or below; use within two days or freeze. Keep raw shrimp separate from ready-to-eat food. Wash hands with soap for 20 seconds after handling raw food and clean utensils and work surfaces. Thaw frozen shrimp in the refrigerator. Cook shrimp to 145°F; flesh should be firm, pearly and opaque. Refrigerate perishables within two hours, or one hour when outdoor temperatures exceed 90°F.

Source: [FDA, Selecting and Serving Fresh and Frozen Seafood Safely](https://www.fda.gov/food/buy-store-serve-safe-food/selecting-and-serving-fresh-and-frozen-seafood-safely?mobile=no). These are general seafood guidelines, not a product-specific shelf-life guarantee. Keep this source alongside published guidance.

## Whole shrimp and flavor

Head-on shrimp give you another way to cook: grill or pan-cook them whole, then peel at the table. Heads and shells can also be used as ingredients in shrimp stock. This is a cooking option, not a claim that head-on shrimp are nutritionally superior.

Culinary source: [Epicurious shrimp stock](https://www.epicurious.com/recipes/food/views/shrimp-stock-383514), which uses heads and shells. No source recipe reproduced.

## Cleaning and peeling

Work with a small chilled batch on a clean board. For head-off shrimp, gently twist the head away where it meets the body. Peel the shell and legs away, leaving the tail if desired. Use a small knife to make a shallow cut along the curved back and lift out the dark intestinal tract. For shell-on cooking, use kitchen shears to open the shell along the back and lift out the tract while leaving the head and shell attached. Pat dry and cook; keep the remaining shrimp refrigerated while working. Wash hands and equipment afterward. ## Lemon-grilled whole shrimp

Ingredients: 1 lb whole head-on shrimp; 1 tablespoon olive oil; 1 lemon; 1 small crushed garlic clove; salt and pepper to taste. Yield depends on actual shrimp size and the proportion of head/shell.

Prepare the shrimp using the shell-on method above. Toss with oil, garlic, a little lemon zest, salt and pepper. Grill over medium-high heat, turning as needed, until the thickest flesh reaches 145°F and is opaque. Use temperature rather than a fixed time because harvest size is unknown. Transfer with clean utensils to a clean platter, add lemon juice and serve with bread or rice. Remove heads and shells at the table. ## Garlic-lemon peeled shrimp

Ingredients: peeled, deveined shrimp from a 1 lb whole package; 1 tablespoon olive oil or butter; 1 small minced garlic clove; half a lemon; chopped parsley and salt to taste. A 1 lb whole package yields less than 1 lb peeled meat; do not label this as 1 lb peeled shrimp.

Prepare using the head-off method. Warm oil or butter in a skillet over medium heat. Add shrimp in a single layer, turning as needed; add garlic near the end so it does not scorch. Cook the thickest flesh to 145°F. Finish with lemon juice and parsley. Serve with rice or pasta. Verify serving yield in the kitchen before publication.
